The Imperial Guard had never been beaten. At half past seven on the evening of Waterloo, it marched up the hill.
Waterloo, evening. The Prussians are pressing in, and Napoleon has only his Guard left.
He sends them.
About five battalions of the Middle Guard climb the slope in tall bearskin hats. Ney marches with them.
They push back the first British brigade in their way. The Prince of Orange falls wounded.
A Dutch brigade charges them with the bayonet.
Then, on the crest, the French see almost nothing. Wellington's Foot Guards are lying flat in the wheat.
On command, they rise up and fire, at point-blank range.
The front ranks of the Guard collapse. A British light infantry regiment, the fifty-second, swings round and charges them from the side.
And the Guard breaks.
A cry runs through the whole French army: "The Guard is retreating! Every man for himself!"
At that moment, Prussian troops arrive on Wellington's left and slam into the French line. The French break and run.
Wellington rises in his stirrups and waves his hat: the whole army advances.
Napoleon's empire is collapsing in the dusk.

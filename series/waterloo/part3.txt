Napoleon made one choice the day before Waterloo that historians still argue about.
After beating the Prussians at Ligny, Napoleon must decide who to finish first.
He tries to do both.
He gives Marshal Grouchy thirty-three thousand men and orders him to follow the Prussians. Napoleon keeps the rest for Wellington.
Grouchy has only just been made a marshal. And he starts late.
The Prussians do not run home. Their chief of staff, Gneisenau, leads them north, to the town of Wavre, to stay close to Wellington.
Seventeenth of June. Napoleon reaches Quatre Bras at one o'clock. Wellington is gone, pulling back to a ridge near the village of Waterloo.
Then the sky opens.
A storm breaks. The roads turn to mud. French cavalry chase the British rearguard through the rain, but cannot pin it down.
That night, both armies sleep in soaked fields, and the rain does not stop.
At dawn, the fields are soaked. Cannon balls will sink instead of bouncing. Horses will slip.
And Blücher has promised Wellington he will come.
So you're Napoleon. Do you attack at first light, in the mud, or do you wait for the ground to dry?

# Waterloo, June 1815 - sources

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Waterloo - Seventh Coalition, outlaw declaration 13 March, army sizes (French ~72-73,000 / 246-252 guns at Waterloo; Wellington ~68,000 / 156 guns, 38% British; Prussians ~50,000 arriving progressively), 15 June crossing near Charleroi, Duchess of Richmond's ball, Quatre Bras, Ligny, Grouchy detached with 33,000 men, 17 June rain and mud, cavalry action at Genappe, Wellington at Mont-Saint-Jean; 18 June: overnight rain, attack postponed for the ground to dry, breakfast at Le Caillou (Napoleon calls Wellington a "bad general", the battle "nothing more than eating breakfast" - possibly morale boosting), Hougoumont attack ~11:00-11:30, Legros breaks the north gate with an axe, Coldstream/Scots Guards close it, the Frenchmen inside killed; grand battery of 80 guns; Prussians sighted ~13:15 near Chapelle-Saint-Lambert; d'Erlon's ~14,000 men vs ~6,000 at ~13:30; Picton killed; Household and Union Brigades charge, Scots Greys take eagle of the 45e Ligne, Ponsonby killed, Uxbridge "I committed a great mistake"; d'Erlon's corps loses ~3,000 incl. 2,000+ prisoners; a little before 16:00 Ney mistakes casualties moving to the rear for a retreat, ~9,000 cavalry in 67 squadrons, Napoleon: "an hour too soon"; squares hold, gunners shelter in squares, 12 assaults; ~18:00 La Haye Sainte falls when the King's German Legion runs out of ammunition; French guns fire canister into squares; "Night or the Prussians must come" (reported); Plancenoit changes hands five times, Young Guard sent, then Old Guard battalions; ~19:30 Middle Guard attack under Ney; Maitland's Guards, lying down, rise and fire point-blank volleys; 52nd charges; "La Garde recule. Sauve qui peut!"; Wellington waves his hat from Copenhagen to signal the general advance; "The Guard dies but does not surrender" attributed to Cambronne, probably never said; Zieten's corps arrives on Wellington's left; Wellington and Blücher meet near La Belle Alliance ~21:00; Napoleon's carriage captured at Genappe; Grouchy fights at Wavre, Gérard urges him to "march to the sound of the guns"; Soult's order reaches Grouchy after 20:00; casualties (French ~25,000 killed/wounded + 8,000 captured; Wellington 17,000; Prussians ~7,000); "the nearest-run thing you ever saw in your life"; abdication proposed 22 June; surrender to Captain Maitland of HMS Bellerophon 15 July; St Helena, died 1821; Clausewitz/Jomini on causes of defeat (Prussian arrival, Grouchy detachment, soft ground delaying attack, earlier attack would likely have won).
- https://www.nam.ac.uk/explore/battle-waterloo (National Army Museum) - 15 June crossing of the Sambre to beat the two armies separately; Ligny: Prussians lose over 20,000, French about half; a French corps marched back and forth between Ligny and Quatre Bras without fighting; Grouchy detached with 33,000, Napoleon keeps 72,000 and 246 guns; Wellington 68,000 and 156 guns; wet ground weakens the bombardment and hampers cavalry; Hougoumont attacked 11:30, held all day; ~13:30 French attack on the left centre, British heavy cavalry routs it, overcharges, is cut down; Ney's cavalry vs squares; ~18:00 La Haye Sainte falls; Napoleon sends troops to Plancenoit instead of reinforcing Ney; ~19:00 Imperial Guard halts, wavers, breaks; French nearly 40,000 casualties, Allies 22,000; retreat all night harried by Prussian cavalry; Uxbridge's leg ("By God, sir, I've lost my leg!" - reported); Wellington: "My heart is broken by the terrible loss..."; exile to St Helena.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days - left Elba 26 Feb 1815 with ~1,000 men (brig Inconstant), landed Golfe-Juan 1 March; Laffrey: "If any of you will shoot his Emperor, here I am", troops join him; 5th Line joins at Grenoble; Ney had promised to bring him back "in an iron cage", joins him 14 March at Lons-le-Saunier with ~6,000 men; Louis XVIII flees; Paris 20 March; outlaw declaration 13 March; four powers pledge 150,000 men each (25 March); contemporaries thought him prematurely aged; hemorrhoids made long periods on horseback painful at Waterloo.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Ligny - 16 June 1815; French ~63,000 vs Prussians ~83,000; Blücher, 72, leading a cavalry charge, horse shot and falls on him, pulled out semi-conscious; Gneisenau takes command, Prussians withdraw north to Wavre, keeping contact with Wellington; d'Erlon's I Corps ordered to Ligny, recalled by Ney, turned back a few km from Ligny, fought in neither battle; Ligny was Napoleon's last victory.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Ney - "bravest of the brave" (Russia 1812 rearguard); iron cage promise and defection; Waterloo: led cavalry charge, guns overrun but not spiked, five horses killed under him, led one of the last infantry charges; executed by firing squad in Paris 7 December 1815; last words disputed.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_de_Grouchy,_Marquis_de_Grouchy - last Marshal created by Napoleon (April 1815); commanded the right wing after Ligny to pursue the Prussians; heard the cannon of Waterloo but kept to his orders; Wavre: tactical victory but Prussian rearguard let main army reach Wellington; brought his force back to Paris intact; long blamed by veterans as a "betrayer".

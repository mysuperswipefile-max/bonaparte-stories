Two days before Waterloo, Napoleon won the last victory of his life. And it still went wrong.
Napoleon has escaped exile, taken back France, and invaded Belgium.
His plan: split them apart, and beat them one at a time.
In Brussels, Wellington is at a ball when news arrives: the French are coming. He sends his army to a crossroads called Quatre Bras.
Sixteenth of June. Napoleon splits his army in two.
Marshal Ney takes the left wing to Quatre Bras. Napoleon takes the rest against the Prussians, at Ligny.
Blücher, seventy-two years old, leads a cavalry charge himself. His horse is shot and falls on top of him. He is dragged out half-conscious.
By nightfall the Prussians have lost more than twenty thousand men. The French, about half that.
But a whole French corps under General d'Erlon spends the day marching between the two battles. Ordered to Ligny, then called back by Ney, it fights nowhere.
So the Prussians are beaten, but not destroyed. And at Quatre Bras, Wellington holds.

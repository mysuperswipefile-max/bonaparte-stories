At six o'clock, Wellington's centre was close to breaking. Napoleon was one push away from victory.
Waterloo, evening. The French cavalry is worn out, and at the village of Plancenoit, Prussians are pushing into Napoleon's rear.
Napoleon sends the Young Guard, eight battalions. They retake the village, then lose it. He sends two battalions of the Old Guard. They take it back with the bayonet.
Plancenoit changes hands five times.
At the centre of Wellington's line stands a farm called La Haye Sainte, held by German soldiers of the King's German Legion.
After fighting all day, they run out of ammunition.
Around six o'clock, the French take the farm.
French guns roll up close and fire into the British squares. Two battered regiments merge into one square to survive.
According to one famous account, Wellington says: "Night or the Prussians must come."
Ney asks for more troops to finish the job. But Napoleon's reserves are being swallowed by Plancenoit.
Wellington uses every minute to patch his line.
You're Napoleon. You have one reserve left: the rest of the Imperial Guard, the men who have never been beaten. Do you throw them in, or keep them back?

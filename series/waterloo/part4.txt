"It will be nothing more than eating breakfast." That is what Napoleon said on the morning of Waterloo.
Eighteenth of June. Napoleon faces Wellington's army on a ridge in Belgium, after a night of rain.
At breakfast, his chief of staff, Soult, suggests calling back Grouchy's men.
Napoleon refuses. He calls Wellington a bad general, and says the battle will be nothing more than eating breakfast.
Maybe he believed it. Maybe he only wanted to keep his officers confident.
Still, he waits for the ground to dry for his cavalry and guns. The morning slips away.
Around half past eleven, the first attack hits a walled farm called Hougoumont.
It is meant to be a feint. It becomes a battle of its own.
A French officer named Legros smashes the north gate with an axe. French soldiers pour into the courtyard.
British Guards force the gate shut. Almost every Frenchman trapped inside is killed.
Hougoumont will hold all day.
Then, around one o'clock, Napoleon sees dark columns on the horizon to his right. Prussians.
You're Napoleon. Do you pull back while you still can, or attack Wellington now, before they arrive?

After three days of slaughter, the battle of Arcole was decided by twenty-five men and a handful of trumpets.
Seventeenth of November. Two days, and the bridge at Arcole still hasn't fallen. But overnight, Napoleon's engineers have built a new bridge further downstream.
At dawn, French troops pour across it and hit the village from a new side.
The Austrians answer with everything they have. By three in the afternoon, a counterattack smashes the French. Men run back toward the river.
For the third day in a row, it looks lost.
But Masséna's men are waiting along the dike. As the Austrians chase the fleeing French, Masséna hits them in the flank.
You're Napoleon. Your attack is collapsing for the third day in a row. Do you throw in your last reserves, or sound the retreat?
Then Napoleon plays his last card. A young lieutenant and twenty-five horsemen ride around the swamp, behind the Austrian lines, with trumpets. Their orders: blow as loud as you can, as if a whole army were charging.
Twenty-five men. A bluff.
The Austrians panic. Around five o'clock, the French storm into Arcole.
Napoleon has won.

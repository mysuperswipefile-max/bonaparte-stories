Napoleon won at Arcole. But the most famous painting of that day hides what really happened.
After three days in the swamp, Arcole has fallen. Napoleon has won.
But walk along those dikes that evening. Thousands of French soldiers killed, wounded or captured. Bodies in the reeds. And somewhere among them, Muiron.
Weeks later in Milan, a painter named Gros paints Napoleon the way the soldiers now tell the story: hair flying, flag in hand, charging onto the bridge.
In truth, he never crossed it. He ended up in a ditch. But the painting shows the courage, not the ditch. And that is the image the world remembers.
Two months later, he crushes Austria's fourth army at Rivoli. Mantua surrenders. Austria makes peace.
The Corsican boy mocked for his accent returns to Paris at twenty-eight, the most famous man in France. Two years later, he rules it.

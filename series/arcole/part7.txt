The swamp was already full of bodies. Napoleon sent his men back in anyway.
Sixteenth of November. Napoleon sends his exhausted army back into the swamp at Arcole.
This time, the Austrians come out to meet them. A column marches down the western dike, straight at the French general Masséna.
Masséna lets them come, then hits them from the front and the side at once. On a dike a few men wide, they have nowhere to go. One of their generals is killed. Five of their cannons are taken.
On the other dike, both sides push each other back and forth all day, over the same few hundred metres of mud.
And Arcole still holds.
You're Napoleon. Two days, thousands of men lost, and the village still holds. Do you try a third day, or cut your losses?
Why keep going? A saying often credited to Napoleon: in war, the moral is to the physical as three is to one. He believes the side that refuses to break, wins.
The second night falls. Nothing has changed on the map.
But in the dark, Napoleon's engineers start building something in secret.

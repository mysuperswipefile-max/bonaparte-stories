Fifteenth of November. Napoleon's army is moving through the swamp toward the village of Arcole.
Between them and the village runs a small river, crossed by one wooden bridge. On the far side, Austrian soldiers wait in the houses, with cannons aimed straight down the dike.
The first French column charges. The Austrians fire. Men are thrown into the black water below. The column breaks.
They try again. And again. One by one, the generals leading them are hit. Even Lannes, one of the bravest officers in the army, goes down.
These soldiers have followed Napoleon from victory to victory. Now they won't move. They press themselves against the side of the dike, frozen.
You're Napoleon, watching your men refuse to move. Do you send another general up there, or do you go yourself?
Napoleon knows that if nobody crosses that bridge, everything he has built dies right here.

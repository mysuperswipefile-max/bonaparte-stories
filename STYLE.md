# Bonaparte Stories: script rules

Language: English. Audience: people who know nothing about Napoleon.

## Series shape
- One battle = one series, about 10 minutes in total, cut into parts of 65-80 seconds (150-180 words). Never under 61 s.
- Stay inside the battle from the first second. Backstory only in short flashes (2-3 lines) at the moment it explains a decision.
- Series title shown on screen (e.g. "WHEN NAPOLEON ALMOST LOST"), with a PART N badge in a different bright colour each part.

## Every part
1. Hook (first line, spoken under the title): one dramatic, TRUE sentence that sets the stakes in 3 seconds and teases this part's payoff (a death, a trick, a reversal) without giving it away.
2. A one-line recap so a new viewer is never lost.
3. Every sentence leads into the next. Explain who the enemy is, why they fight, what is at stake, in plain words. Never assume context.
4. Show, don't tell: vivid sensory details (rain, mud, smoke, cold), then the emotion.
5. Napoleon's psychology: why he chose what he chose (his reading of Alexander and Caesar, his Corsican outsider past, his ambition) so the viewer understands him from the inside.
6. One viewer question, asked at a decision point BEFORE revealing Napoleon's choice: "You're Napoleon. Do you X, or Y?" Always Napoleon's real dilemma, never a modern-life analogy.
7. Cliffhanger ending, then exactly: "Part <next> is next. Follow so you don't miss it." The last part ends with a comment question and "Follow for the next battle."
- No modern business or self-help lessons.

## Facts
- Facts only. Use at least 2 independent sources per battle (Wikipedia + one other). List them in series/<name>/sources.md.
- A single-source anecdote or legend must be flagged in the script ("according to one famous account").
- Before rendering, a second agent fact-checks each part against the sources and removes anything unsupported.

## Visuals
- Public-domain only (museum open-access collections: Rijksmuseum via pipeline/rijks.py, Art Institute of Chicago, Wikimedia Commons). Never photographs, never modern images, never images of the wrong era.
- No image appears twice in the same video. 5-6 s smooth zoom per shot, soft crossfades.
- Maps drawn with pipeline/maps.py for geography and troop movements.
